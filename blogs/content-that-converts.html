Content That Converts: Writing Copy That Actually Sells

Harpreet Singh (Brand Strategy) February 15, 2026 • 8 Min Read

Most brand copy sounds exactly the same. "Innovative solutions for modern businesses." It's invisible to the reader. To actually sell, your copy needs to break the pattern and speak directly to the psychology of the buyer. Here is the seven-part framework we use across thousands of landing pages to routinely beat industry conversion benchmarks.

1. The "Agitate" Formula

Before you offer the solution, you must make the reader painfully aware of their problem. We use the PAS (Problem, Agitation, Solution) framework. Describe the problem, twist the knife by describing the negative downstream effects of that problem, and *only then* present your product as the relief.

2. Benefit over Feature

A feature is: "This software has a 256-bit encrypted cloud storage system." A benefit is: "Your data is safe from hackers even if your laptop is stolen." Buyers do not care about how your product works; they only care about how it improves their life.

3. The Power of Micro-Copy

Your main headlines matter, but the micro-copy surrounding your forms and buttons dictates whether someone actually converts. Adding a simple line of text under a submit button like "No credit card required. Setup takes 2 minutes." acts as a frictionless ramp that actively removes buying objections.

4. Specificity Beats Vague Claims

Vague copy reads as marketing noise; specific copy reads as truth. "We help businesses grow" is forgettable. "We took a D2C skincare brand from £8k to £61k monthly revenue in 90 days" is impossible to ignore. Numbers, timeframes, and concrete nouns act as proof-of-competence — the more precise the claim, the more believable it becomes, even before the reader has seen any evidence.

Whenever you write a line, ask: could a competitor say the exact same sentence? If yes, it's too generic. Replace adjectives ("fast", "affordable", "reliable") with quantified proof ("live in 14 days", "from £299", "99.4% uptime").

5. Social Proof at the Point of Decision

Testimonials buried on a separate page do almost nothing. Social proof works when it sits exactly where doubt appears — next to the price, beside the signup button, under the headline. A relevant one-line quote from a customer in the same industry as the reader removes more friction than three paragraphs of your own claims.

  • Match the objection: place a "worried about setup?" testimonial next to the onboarding section.
  • Show the face: a name, role and company out-convert an anonymous "— Happy Customer" every time.
  • Quantify the result: "cut our cost-per-lead 40%" beats "great to work with".

6. The One-Reader Rule

Great copy never addresses "businesses" or "users" — it speaks to one specific person. Before writing a page, define that reader precisely: their role, their frustration this morning, the exact phrase they'd type into Google at 11pm. Then write as if replying to their email. Copy written for everyone converts no one; copy written for one person converts thousands who recognise themselves in it.

7. A Single, Unmissable Call to Action

Every page should ask the reader to do exactly one thing. Competing CTAs ("Buy now", "Learn more", "Contact us", "Download") split attention and lower action rates. Pick the single most valuable next step, repeat it consistently, and make the button copy describe the outcome ("Get my free audit") rather than the mechanic ("Submit"). Decision fatigue is the silent killer of conversion — remove every choice that isn't the one you want them to make.

Putting the Framework Together

These seven principles compound. Agitate the real problem, sell the benefit not the feature, smooth the path with micro-copy, prove it with specifics and social proof, write to one person, and point them at one clear action. None of it requires clever wordplay — just discipline and an obsessive focus on the reader. Test one variable at a time, keep what wins, and your pages will quietly outperform competitors who are still writing "innovative solutions for modern businesses".

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes marketing copy convert?

Copy converts when it speaks to one specific reader's problem, sells the benefit rather than the feature, backs claims with specifics and social proof, and points to a single clear action — clarity and relevance beat cleverness every time.

What is the PAS copywriting formula?

Problem, Agitation, Solution: name the reader's problem, make them feel its real cost, then present your product as the relief. It is one of the most reliable structures for persuasive marketing copy.

How do I write a strong call to action?

Ask for one thing, describe the outcome not the mechanic ('Get my free audit' beats 'Submit'), and remove competing links so attention is not split. Reduce every point of friction around the action you want.
