01
The local pack problem

Three slots decide the market, and proximity alone will not get you into them

For any query with local intent, Google shows a map and three business listings before a single organic result. Those three capture the calls, the direction requests and the walk-ins. The ranking model behind them — relevance, distance and prominence — is only partly about how close you are, which is exactly why businesses further from the searcher routinely outrank nearer ones.

An under-built Business Profile

Missing categories, thin service lists, no products, unanswered Q&A and stale photos all suppress relevance scoring. The profile is a ranking surface in its own right, not a directory entry you fill in once and forget.

Review volume without velocity

A static pile of old five-star reviews reads as dormant. The model responds to recency, cadence, keyword content and whether you reply — a steady monthly flow beats a large historic total.

Inconsistent NAP across the web

Conflicting names, addresses and phone numbers across directories and aggregators dilute entity confidence. When Google cannot resolve which record is authoritative, it discounts all of them.

No location-level content

One generic services page cannot compete for a dozen service areas. Without genuinely differentiated location pages carrying real local proof, you have nothing to rank in the areas you are not physically in.

02
What Maps SEO covers

Organic SEO ranks a page. Maps SEO ranks a business entity.

Google Maps SEO — local pack or Business Profile optimisation — is the work of making Google confident about three things: what your business does, where it legitimately serves, and whether real people vouch for it.

The mechanics are different from organic SEO because the ranked object is different. You are not optimising a URL; you are strengthening an entity in Google’s local index, then giving it enough corroboration across the web and enough on-site local content to be trusted for each area you claim.

  • Business Profile engineering — primary and secondary categories chosen from competitive analysis, complete service and product catalogues, attributes, hours, geotagged imagery and an actively managed Q&A section.
  • Local keyword architecture — service-plus-area mapping across every location you serve, with intent segmented into emergency, comparison and research so each gets the page format it needs.
  • Review acquisition systems — compliant, automated request flows that generate steady monthly velocity with natural service-keyword language, plus structured response handling for every review.
  • Citation & NAP cleanup — audit and correction across primary directories, industry verticals and the data aggregators that quietly repopulate bad records after you fix them.
  • Location page builds — genuinely differentiated pages per service area with local proof, embedded maps, service-area schema and internal linking that reinforces geographic relevance.

What is changing through 2026

Four shifts in how local intent gets resolved

The local surface is getting richer and more automated at once. Profiles now carry far more ranking weight, and AI-generated local answers are starting to sit above the pack itself.

01

The profile is becoming the landing page

More local journeys now complete inside the Business Profile — messages, bookings, product browsing, direction requests — without ever reaching your website. Profile completeness is shifting from a ranking factor to the conversion surface itself.

02

AI local answers sit above the pack

Generated summaries increasingly answer “best X near me” before the map appears, synthesising review sentiment and profile data. Being well-reviewed and well-structured now feeds a layer that did not exist two years ago.

03

Review content matters more than review count

Systems parse what reviews actually say, matching service language and sentiment to query intent. Twenty reviews describing specific services now outperform two hundred that only say “great service”.

04

Spam enforcement is tightening unevenly

Fake listings and keyword-stuffed business names still distort competitive markets, but enforcement is improving. Systematic reporting has become a legitimate and increasingly effective part of local strategy.

How does Google decide local pack rankings?+

Three inputs: relevance (how well your business matches the query), distance (proximity to the searcher or the searched area) and prominence (how well-known and well-corroborated you are across the web and in reviews). Distance is fixed, but relevance and prominence are both buildable — which is why a well-optimised business further away regularly outranks a nearer one.

Can I rank in areas where I don’t have an address?+

Yes, though it is harder and requires real substance. Service-area businesses can rank beyond their physical location by combining correct service-area configuration, genuinely differentiated location pages carrying local proof, and prominence signals that establish you as known in that area. What does not work is thin doorway pages with the town name swapped — those get filtered and can trigger a penalty.

How many reviews do I need?+

Velocity and content matter more than a total. A steady flow of a handful of recent reviews each month, describing specific services in natural language, outperforms a large but stale historic pile. Practically, most competitive local markets need a rating above 4.2 to clear buyer filtering, then consistent monthly additions to hold prominence.

Why did my rankings drop after I edited my profile?+

Category changes, name edits and address changes all trigger re-evaluation, and some edits queue for manual review. Short-term volatility after a significant change is normal and usually settles within a few weeks. Persistent drops more often trace to a category chosen incorrectly, a guideline breach, or a competitor gaining ground — which is why we document every edit and grid-scan before and after.

How long does Maps SEO take?+

Faster than organic SEO. Profile and citation corrections often move rankings within two to six weeks. Review velocity and prominence build over three to six months, and expansion into additional service areas typically shows through around months four to eight. Highly contested urban categories sit at the slower end of that range.

Do I need location pages if I have a Business Profile?+

For a single-location business serving one area, the profile carries most of the weight. The moment you claim multiple service areas you need genuinely differentiated pages, because Google looks for on-site evidence that you actually operate in each. They must carry real local content — projects, staff, specifics — not templated text with the place name substituted.

What can be done about competitors with fake listings?+

Quite a lot. Fake addresses, keyword-stuffed business names and listings at virtual offices all breach guidelines and can be reported through the redressal process. We document violations with evidence and submit them systematically. In contested categories, removing spam listings is frequently the single fastest ranking gain available, ahead of any optimisation work.

Is Maps SEO different from Local SEO?+

They overlap heavily and are often used interchangeably. In practice we treat Maps SEO as the profile-and-pack work — entity strength, reviews, citations, grid coverage — while Local SEO also covers ranking your website organically for local queries. Most engagements need both, since the pack and the local organic results are won with different assets.
