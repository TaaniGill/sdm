How we keep design consistent at volume: shared type scale, defined colour tokens, a working grid and export presets for every destination.

01 — The brief

The bottleneck is rarely design talent. It is the system.

Most businesses do not need better-looking work so much as a way to produce good work repeatedly, quickly, without a designer redrawing the same layout each time. The teams that struggle are almost always producing every asset from scratch.

What we get asked for
  • “Can you make this look better?”
  • “We need it by tomorrow”
  • “Just resize it for all the platforms”
  • “Make it pop”
  • “Use the logo bigger”
What actually decides the outcome
  • Whether the message is legible in the two seconds it will actually get
  • Whether it reads on a phone screen, which is where most of it is seen
  • Whether your team can produce the next twenty versions without us
  • Whether it looks like it came from the same company as last month
  • Whether the file is correctly set up for print, screen or paid placement

The thing that changes output most: a template system. Once type scale, grid, colour tokens and export presets are fixed, producing a campaign becomes assembly rather than invention — which is how turnaround drops from days to hours without quality falling.

04 — Standards

What we check before anything ships

Most design problems that reach a customer are technical rather than aesthetic — and they are entirely preventable.

Legible at the size it will be seen

Work is reviewed at real dimensions on a phone, not zoomed on a desktop. A design that only works at full size fails in the feed, which is where nearly all of it is actually encountered.

Correct setup for its destination

Bleed, crop marks and CMYK for print; RGB, dimensions and text-safe areas for screen. Sending an RGB file to a printer or an oversized text overlay to an ad platform wastes budget in ways nobody notices until it is live.

Accessible contrast

Text checked against background contrast ratios. Low-contrast type looks refined in a review and becomes unreadable on a phone in daylight, which is the actual viewing condition.

Built from the system

Assets assembled from defined tokens and components rather than improvised each time. It is what keeps a hundred pieces looking related without policing every one.

Source files supplied

Layered, organised, named files handed over as standard. Withholding source to secure future work is common and something we will not do.

Fonts and images properly licensed

Commercial licences confirmed for every typeface and stock asset. Unlicensed use is genuinely enforceable and lands on the client, not the designer.

05 — What is changing

Four shifts in commercial design

Volume expectations have risen sharply while attention has fallen. Both change how work should be produced.

01
Platforms need volume to optimise

Automated ad delivery needs creative variety to test against. A handful of assets per quarter starves it, so systematic production has become a performance factor rather than a nice-to-have.

02
Vertical and mobile-first is the default

Most creative is now consumed vertically on a phone. Landscape work repurposed by cropping consistently underperforms assets designed for the format from the start.

03
Generative tools changed the starting point

AI handles some ideation and variation quickly. What it does not do is judge whether a message lands, keep a brand coherent, or set a file up correctly for print — so the value moved to direction and craft.

04
Accessibility expectations reached marketing

Contrast, caption legibility and alt text are increasingly expected on commercial creative, not just on interfaces, and some platforms now surface it as a quality signal.

08 — Questions

What clients ask before commissioning design work

Practical answers on pricing, turnaround, files and working with your team.

Do you work on projects or retainers?

Both. Projects suit defined pieces of work with a clear end — a campaign, a packaging run, a deck. Retainers suit businesses producing creative continuously, where the value comes from the system and the speed it enables rather than from any single asset. We will recommend whichever matches your actual volume.

How fast can you turn work around?

Two to five working days per asset from an approved brief, faster once a template system exists. What delays projects is rarely production; it is briefs that change mid-way or approval spread across several people without a single decision-maker.

Do we get the source files?

Yes, always, layered and organised. Some studios withhold source files so clients must return for every change. We hand them over as standard, along with editable templates where relevant, because holding files hostage is not a business model we are interested in.

Can you work with our existing brand guidelines?

Yes, and we prefer it. If guidelines exist we build to them exactly. If they are thin or being ignored we will say so and suggest tightening the system first, since producing volume against unclear rules just multiplies the inconsistency.

How many revisions are included?

Two rounds as standard, which is usually sufficient when the brief was clear. Additional rounds are chargeable, but in practice the projects needing many revisions are the ones where the brief was vague or the decision-maker was not in the first conversation — so we spend time on the brief.

Do you use AI in your design work?

For some ideation and variation, where it genuinely speeds things up. It does not replace judgement about whether a message lands, brand coherence, or correct technical setup for print and platforms. Everything is directed and checked by a designer, and we will tell you where it was used if you ask.

Can you handle print production too?

We supply print-ready artwork with correct bleed, colour profiles and resolution, and we will pre-flight before submission. We can liaise with your printer or recommend one, though you contract them directly so you keep control of cost and quality.
