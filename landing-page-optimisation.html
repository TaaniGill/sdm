01
Why pages underconvert

The traffic is fine. The page is asking too much, too early

Most underperforming landing pages fail for the same handful of reasons: the page does not say what the ad said, the form asks for more than the offer is worth, it is slow on the phone where most of the traffic is, and nothing on it makes an unfamiliar company look safe to contact. None of those are fixed by buying more traffic.

No message match

The ad promised something specific and the page shows a generic homepage. The visitor cannot tell they are in the right place and leaves.

Forms that ask too much

Eleven fields for a first enquiry. Every additional field costs completions, and most of them are never used by anyone.

Slow on mobile

Field performance rather than a lab score. Most paid traffic is mobile and a page that takes seconds to become usable loses the visit before it is read.

No reason to trust

No reviews, no real photography, no address, no named people. For an unfamiliar company that absence is enough to stop an enquiry.

02
How we do it

Fix the obvious, then test the rest.

A meaningful share of the gain comes from things that do not need testing at all — matching the page to the ad, cutting the form to what you actually use, making it fast on a phone, and putting genuine proof where the decision happens.

Then structured testing on what remains, with enough traffic to reach a real result rather than calling a winner after two days on a difference that is noise.

  • Message match — the page reflects the ad or query that brought them, so there is no gap between what was promised and what appears.
  • Form reduction — every field justified against whether anyone uses it. Progressive capture for the rest, asked once there is a relationship.
  • Mobile performance — measured on field data at the 75th percentile, because that is what visitors experience and what Google uses.
  • Proof where the decision happens — reviews, real photography, credentials and named people placed next to the call to action rather than buried on an about page.
  • Structured testing — one meaningful change at a time, run to significance, with results documented so nothing gets retested a year later.

The method

How pages get rebuilt

Six steps. The first three usually deliver most of the improvement.

01

Behaviour analysis

Where people stop — scroll depth, field-level drop-off, rage clicks and session recordings on the pages that matter commercially.

02

Message match audit

Every ad and query mapped against the page it lands on, to find the gaps between what was promised and what appears.

03

Performance fixes

Core Web Vitals on field data, with the specific elements causing failures identified rather than a general speed pass.

04

Form rebuild

Fields cut to what is genuinely used, validation made forgiving, and the submission path tested on real devices.

05

Trust placement

Genuine proof positioned next to the action rather than on a separate page nobody visits.

06

Testing programme

Structured tests on offer, headline and structure, run to significance and documented so learning accumulates.

04
What is changing

Four things that changed conversion work

Measurement got harder and mobile got more decisive.

01

INP replaced FID

Interaction to Next Paint measures every interaction rather than the first. Pages that felt fine on the old metric frequently fail this one, and it correlates with abandonment.

02

Consent modes cut observable data

Fewer sessions are measurable, so tests need longer to reach significance and small differences are harder to trust than they used to be.

03

Mobile share kept rising

In most paid campaigns the majority of traffic is mobile, which makes desktop-first design decisions actively expensive.

04

Trust signals matter more

With more AI-generated sites competing, visible evidence of a real business — named people, genuine photography, verifiable reviews — does more work than it used to.

Questions

Questions about landing page optimisation

On forms, testing, mobile and what actually moves conversion.

Related services

What is landing page optimisation?+

Improving the share of visitors to a page who take the action it exists for. In practice that means matching the page to what brought the visitor, reducing what the form asks, making it fast on mobile, and placing genuine proof where the decision is made — then testing what remains.

Why optimise pages instead of buying more traffic?+

Because it is usually cheaper and the gain applies everywhere at once. Going from 2% to 4% conversion halves your cost per lead across every channel permanently, with no increase in media spend. Buying the same outcome through traffic means doubling the budget indefinitely.

How many form fields should we have?+

As few as you genuinely use. Every additional field costs completions, and most forms ask for information nobody ever acts on. Ask for what you need to have a first conversation and collect the rest once there is a relationship. Cutting fields is frequently the single largest gain available on a page.

How long does A/B testing take?+

Longer than most people allow. You need enough conversions to reach significance, which on lower-traffic pages can mean weeks per test. Calling a winner after two days on a small difference is reading noise as signal, and it is why so many reported conversion wins do not survive contact with reality.

What if we do not have enough traffic to test?+

Then testing is the wrong tool and we will say so. On low-traffic pages the gains come from fixing the known problems — message match, form length, mobile speed, trust signals — none of which require statistical validation because they are not close calls.

Should landing pages be separate from the main site?+

For paid campaigns, usually yes, so the page matches the ad exactly and carries no competing navigation. For organic, the page needs to serve a broader intent and sit properly within the site. The two jobs are different and a single page rarely does both well.

Do you rebuild pages or just advise?+

Either. We can hand your developer a specification detailed enough to implement without a follow-up call, or build and test the pages ourselves. Most clients start with the specification and move to us building once the value is proven.

How quickly will we see improvement?+

Faster than most marketing work. Message match and form fixes can show within days of shipping because they need no algorithmic response — just a better page. Structured testing then compounds over the following months.
