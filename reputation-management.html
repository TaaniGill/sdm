Online Reputation Management

You cannot delete the internet.
You can change what it shows.

Reputation management is not about removing criticism. It is about earning enough genuine positive signal that a fair picture is what people find — and about responding well when something goes wrong.

What we manage: rating distribution rather than the headline average, a response workflow with real deadlines, and how much of page one you control.

01 — The brief

Almost everyone asks us to remove a review first

It is the natural instinct and it is usually the wrong goal. Reviews only come down if they breach platform policy, and chasing removal wastes the weeks that would be better spent making the negative one statistically irrelevant. A single one-star review among two hundred reads as bad luck; among nine, it reads as a pattern.

What we get asked for
  • “Can you get this review taken down?”
  • “Can we bury it in the search results?”
  • “Can you post some positive reviews for us?”
  • “Our competitor is leaving fake reviews”
  • “We need this gone by next week”
What actually decides the outcome
  • How many genuine reviews you collect each month, consistently
  • How quickly and how well you respond in public
  • Whether the underlying complaint is actually recurring
  • How much of page one for your brand name you control
  • Whether staff know what to do when something goes wrong

What we will not do: write fake reviews, buy testimonials, or incentivise positive ratings. It breaches platform terms and consumer protection law in most of the markets we work in, it is increasingly detectable, and the penalty — a public warning label on your profile — is far worse than the reviews you were worried about.

04 — Standards

How we work, and what we refuse to do

This is the service where the temptation to cheat is strongest and the consequences of being caught are most public.

Only genuine reviews, always

We never write, buy or incentivise reviews. Beyond being against platform terms and consumer protection law in most markets, detection now results in a visible warning on your profile — far more damaging than any review it was meant to hide.

Request from everyone, not just happy customers

Selectively asking only satisfied customers is review gating, which breaches platform policy. Asking everyone consistently produces a higher average anyway, because satisfied customers outnumber unhappy ones in most businesses.

Respond to negatives properly

Acknowledge, take it offline, resolve, and never argue publicly. Future customers read the response more closely than the complaint — a defensive reply does more damage than the review itself.

Report the themes, not just the score

Recurring complaints are operational intelligence. We feed them back plainly, including when the honest conclusion is that the problem is not a reputation problem.

Speed over polish

A good response within hours beats a perfect one next week. Templates and approval routes exist so responses are fast without being careless.

No suppression theatre

We build legitimate assets that deserve to rank. We do not spin up low-quality sites to push results down — it is transparent to anyone looking and rarely holds.

05 — What is changing

Four shifts in online reputation

Where reputation is formed, and how quickly it is judged, has moved considerably.

01
AI summaries now condense your reviews

Assistants and search summaries increasingly generate a sentence about a business from aggregate review text. Recurring complaint themes get surfaced directly, which raises the cost of unresolved patterns.

02
Recency weighs more heavily

Platforms and customers both weight recent reviews far above old ones. A strong historical average with nothing new reads as a business that has stopped caring.

03
Fake review enforcement has real teeth

Regulators in several markets now impose penalties directly, and platforms apply public warning labels. The risk calculation on buying reviews has changed completely.

04
Response rate became a visible signal

Unanswered reviews are conspicuous, and answering is now read as a proxy for how a business treats customers generally — often more telling than the rating itself.

08 — Questions

What businesses ask about reputation management

Honest answers, including on the things we will not do.

Can you remove a negative review?

Only if it breaches platform policy — fake, defamatory, containing personal information, or posted by someone who was never a customer. We will flag those and pursue them. An honest negative review from a real customer will not be removed, and pursuing it wastes time better spent on generating genuine volume that puts it in proportion.

Can you write positive reviews for us?

No. Writing, buying or incentivising reviews breaches platform terms and consumer protection law in most markets we operate in. Detection now results in a public warning label on your profile, which is considerably more damaging than the reviews you were trying to offset. We will decline this every time.

How long before we see improvement?

Response management and profile fixes show within thirty days. Rating movement depends on your customer volume — a business serving hundreds monthly shifts an average far faster than one serving a dozen. We model the realistic curve from your actual volume during the audit rather than quoting a generic timeframe.

Should we ask every customer for a review?

Yes. Asking only customers you expect to be happy is review gating and breaches platform policy. It also tends to be unnecessary: in most businesses satisfied customers substantially outnumber unhappy ones, so asking everyone consistently produces a better average than filtering.

What do we do when a review is unfair?

Respond once, briefly, without arguing. Acknowledge the experience, state any correction factually and without heat, and offer to resolve it directly. Future customers judge the response more than the complaint, and a calm reply to an unfair review often reads better than a positive review does.

Can you push down a bad search result?

Sometimes, legitimately — by building genuine assets that deserve to rank for your name. We will not create low-quality sites purely to displace results; it is transparent, rarely durable, and can itself become the story. If a result is factually wrong, the route is correction or legal action rather than suppression.

What if the complaints are actually justified?

Then we will tell you plainly, and reputation management is the wrong purchase. Recurring complaint themes are operational feedback, and no review strategy fixes a service problem that keeps generating genuine dissatisfaction. We would rather lose the engagement than take a monthly fee to manage the symptom.
