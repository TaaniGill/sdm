01
The student journey

Students research for months and apply to a handful

Education decisions are slow, high-stakes and heavily researched. Prospective students explore careers, compare qualifications, check funding and read outcomes long before applying. Providers absent from that research are absent from the shortlist.

1
Career

What do I want to do?

Exploring careers and what qualifications they require. Months before any application, and almost entirely unowned by providers.

“how to become a [profession]”
“careers in [sector]”
2
Qualification

What do I need to study?

Understanding which qualification, level and route suits them. Comparison of pathways rather than institutions.

“level 3 vs level 4 [subject]”
“apprenticeship or degree”
3
Provider

Where should I study?

Comparing providers on outcomes, cost, location, mode and reputation. This is where course page depth wins.

“[course] courses near me”
“best [subject] college [city]”
4
Apply

Cost and entry

Funding, entry requirements, start dates and application process. Practical clarity converts research into an application.

“[course] funding available”
“[course] entry requirements”
02
Where providers lose students

Why good courses go unfilled

Education has an unusually long research window and unusually high stakes. Providers who only appear when someone searches their institution by name are entering a decision that was largely made elsewhere.

Thin course pages

Course pages are often a title, a paragraph and a bullet list. That cannot rank against providers publishing genuine curriculum, outcome and progression detail.

Course-level search is where students choose

No outcome transparency

Students want to know completion rates, employment outcomes and progression. Providers publishing nothing lose to those who publish it honestly.

Outcome data is the deciding comparison

Funding questions unanswered

Cost and funding eligibility are the biggest practical barriers. Unanswered, they stop applications from people who would have qualified for support.

Funding uncertainty blocks applications

No career-stage content

Career exploration happens months before course search and is owned entirely by careers publishers rather than the providers who deliver the training.

Entire discovery phase handed away

04
The competitive landscape

Who owns education search

Education search is dominated by comparison portals and careers publishers who sit between prospective students and the institutions actually delivering the courses.

Course comparison portals

UCAS, FindAMasters, Reed Courses and similar rank for nearly every course query, then charge providers for listings or leads.

How we beat them: genuine course depth and outcome data portals cannot hold.

Careers publishers

Careers advice sites own the discovery phase where students first explore professions and required qualifications.

How we beat them: practical route guidance tied to courses you actually deliver.

Large institutions

Universities and national training providers with substantial marketing budgets and established brand recognition.

How we beat them: specialist focus, flexibility and stronger local or sector reputation.

Common questions

Questions education providers ask

Practical answers on course pages, outcome data and competing with comparison portals.

How do we compete with comparison portals?+

By offering the depth they structurally cannot hold. Portals rank on breadth and domain authority but carry only shallow course summaries. Students comparing seriously want curriculum detail, assessment methods, real outcome data and progression routes — and increasingly prefer a provider’s own detailed page to a thin listing.

Should we publish outcome data?+

Yes, and honestly — including where results are only average. Students and parents are actively looking for it, and providers publishing nothing are assumed to be hiding something. Transparent outcome data is consistently one of the most persuasive things an education provider can publish, and it is genuinely rare.

Is career content worth it if it does not convert directly?+

For education, yes. Career exploration happens months before course search and is where the shortlist actually forms. Being present during discovery gets you considered. We measure it as assisted conversion rather than last-click, because judging it on direct applications badly undervalues it in a six-month research cycle.

Our courses are online. Does local SEO matter?+

Much less, and the strategy changes. Online delivery removes geographic constraints, so we target course and qualification queries nationally rather than concentrating locally. Many providers run both campus and online provision, and those genuinely need separate pages targeting different query patterns.

How important is funding content?+

It is one of the biggest practical unlocks. Cost and funding eligibility are the main barriers stopping qualified people from applying, and the rules are complicated enough that most prospective students do not know what they are entitled to. Explaining eligibility clearly converts research into applications from people who would otherwise have self-excluded.

How long before enrolments increase?+

Course page rankings typically build over two to four months. Because education is cyclical, revenue impact depends on where you sit relative to application deadlines — content must rank before the window opens. We plan the calendar backwards from your recruitment cycles rather than working to a generic timeline.

How do you measure results?+

Enrolment enquiries and applications by course, attributed to organic search, plus assisted conversions from discovery content. We report by course because filling an undersubscribed course is worth far more than adding applicants to one already at capacity.
