01
The buyer journey

Luxury buyers research provenance, not price

High-value purchases are researched carefully but on different criteria. Authenticity, provenance, craftsmanship and aftercare matter far more than price comparison. Brands applying standard ecommerce SEO tactics frequently damage positioning while chasing the wrong queries.

The cost of the wrong approach

Why standard ecommerce SEO damages luxury brands

Most ecommerce SEO tactics are built around price competition and volume. Applied to luxury they attract the wrong buyers, erode positioning and frequently reduce revenue despite increasing traffic.

Chasing discount queries

Ranking for cheap and sale queries attracts buyers who will not convert at your price point and signals to the market that you compete on discount.

Traffic rises, positioning and margin fall

No authenticity content

Counterfeit anxiety is the dominant concern in luxury purchase. Brands not addressing authentication lose buyers to sellers who do.

Authenticity concern blocks high-value sales

No provenance or craftsmanship story

Luxury buyers research why something costs what it does. Without craftsmanship and heritage content there is nothing justifying the price.

Price becomes unjustified without the story

No clienteling path

High-value buyers frequently want a conversation before purchasing. Sites offering only add-to-cart lose them at the final step.

Highest-value buyers need a human path

04
The competitive landscape

Who competes for luxury buyers

Luxury search is contested by official brand sites, high-end marketplaces and a resale sector that has professionalised rapidly.

Brand-owned ecommerce

Maisons and houses selling direct with enormous brand authority and complete control over their own name searches.

How we beat them: curation, availability, pre-owned access and personal service.

Luxury marketplaces

Net-a-Porter, Farfetch, Vestiaire and similar with large budgets and established trust in the resale and multi-brand space.

How we beat them: authentication depth, provenance detail and genuine clienteling.

Grey market and counterfeits

Unauthorised sellers competing on price, and counterfeit listings that make buyers anxious across the whole category.

How we beat them: verifiable authenticity, certification and authorised status.

Common questions

Questions luxury brands ask

Direct answers on positioning, authenticity and why volume is the wrong metric here.

Will SEO damage our brand positioning?+

Badly executed SEO will. Chasing discount and cheap queries attracts buyers who will never convert at your price point and signals that you compete on price. Done properly, SEO reinforces positioning by making provenance, craftsmanship and authenticity visible — which is what actually justifies premium pricing to a researching buyer.

Our search volumes are tiny. Is it worth it?+

Volume is the wrong measure in luxury. A query with fifty monthly searches can produce orders worth thousands each, and everyone searching it is a serious buyer. We evaluate opportunity by order value and buyer quality, which is why we frequently recommend targeting queries a standard SEO tool would dismiss.

How important is authenticity content?+

It is the single biggest unlock in luxury ecommerce. Counterfeit anxiety is the dominant concern for anyone spending significantly online, particularly in pre-owned. Authentication guides, certification detail and verifiable authorised status directly remove the barrier that stops those transactions.

Should we target pre-owned and resale queries?+

In most categories yes — resale has professionalised rapidly, margins are strong and competition is lighter than in new. It also captures buyers earlier in their relationship with a category, many of whom later buy new. The requirement is genuinely rigorous authentication, because trust is the whole proposition.

Do we need ecommerce, or are appointments enough?+

Both, and the mistake is offering only one. Some high-value buyers transact online; many want a conversation first. Sites offering only add-to-cart lose the second group entirely. We build clienteling paths — private appointments, consultations, personal shopping — alongside standard purchase.

How do we compete with the brand's own website?+

Not on brand-name searches — the house will always own those. You compete on curation, availability, pre-owned access, cross-brand comparison and personal service. Multi-brand retailers also win on occasion and gifting queries that individual houses do not target.

How do you measure results?+

Order value and revenue rather than traffic, plus enquiry quality and appointment requests. We deliberately report unqualified traffic reduction as a positive outcome, because in luxury attracting the wrong visitors costs more than it appears in a standard analytics view.
