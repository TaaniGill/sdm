The Indian market

What makes search in India different

These are the things that change strategy here — not generic SEO advice with a country name attached.

Mobile-first, overwhelmingly

The vast majority of Indian search happens on mobile, often on mid-range devices over variable connections. Core Web Vitals is not a nice-to-have here — a site that fails mobile performance is invisible to most of the market.

Multilingual intent

Buyers frequently search in transliterated Hinglish or a regional script. Targeting English alone leaves substantial volume uncontested, and direct translation misses how people actually phrase queries.

Voice and vernacular growth

Voice search adoption is high and growing, particularly outside metro areas. Conversational, question-shaped content wins disproportionately.

Price-sensitive comparison

Indian buyers compare more extensively and expect pricing transparency earlier in the journey than in the US or UK.

How we work

A fully remote team, deliberately

In your timezone, not ours

Calls, reporting and escalation are scheduled around India business hours. Remote does not mean asynchronous — it means you are not funding an office you will never visit.

Specialists, not a local generalist

Your account is worked by whoever is genuinely strongest at the discipline you need rather than whoever happens to sit in a India office. For technical SEO and digital PR that difference is substantial.

No premises overhead in your fee

Everything documented

Remote delivery enforces discipline co-located teams often skip — written specifications, recorded decisions, itemised work logs. You always know what was done and why.

Market conditions

How search actually behaves in India

Hinglish and transliterated queries are enormous and largely invisible in English-only keyword tools. Voice search adoption runs ahead of Western markets, People Also Ask is unusually dense, and Google Discover sends meaningful traffic to publishers here in a way it does not in most markets.

Platforms that hold the SERP

IndiaMART dominates B2B discovery and frequently outranks manufacturer sites. JustDial and Sulekha still matter for local services, Practo for healthcare, 99acres and MagicBricks for property, Zomato and Swiggy for food. A category strategy that ignores these is fighting for the scraps beneath them.

Who you are competing with

India has the largest and cheapest agency market in the world, and price is anchored far below what serious work costs. Offers of ₹10,000–15,000 a month are everywhere, almost always funded by bulk directory submissions and spun content. The real competition for commercial terms is not those agencies — it is well-funded domestic platforms like IndiaMART, JustDial and the vertical aggregators, which occupy most of page one for category queries.

Cost

What SEO costs in India

Serious engagements run roughly ₹25,000–₹1,50,000 per month depending on competitiveness, plus 18% GST. Be wary of anyone quoting per keyword — it is the clearest sign the work will be volume link building rather than anything structural. Enterprise and BFSI budgets sit well above this band.

Privacy & compliance

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 governs consent and data handling. Enforcement is still maturing, but consent notices and purpose limitation are now statutory rather than optional.

Demand cycles

The festive quarter around Diwali is the commercial peak for retail and consumer categories, with buying research starting six to eight weeks earlier. Wedding season drives jewellery, travel and services. Education follows the admission and exam calendar rather than the retail one.

Avoid this

The mistake we see most often in India

Targeting English head terms only. A furniture retailer optimising for ‘sofa set online’ misses the far larger volume searching in transliterated Hindi and regional scripts, and the mobile experience on a mid-range Android over patchy 4G decides whether any of that traffic converts.
